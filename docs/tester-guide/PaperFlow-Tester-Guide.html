PaperFlow

Question papers that set themselves, and practice that comes from each student's own mistakes.

Thank you for testing PaperFlow. This guide walks you through the whole website as the four kinds of people who use it: a teacher, a student, the institute's owner, and us (the people who run PaperFlow and look after the question bank).

For each part it tells you what to try and why that feature exists. What we most want is your honest opinion: what is useful, what is confusing, what is missing, and what your teachers and students would actually use.

You will need
  • The website link that Sanskar sent you with this guide.
  • A computer, for the teacher and owner parts.
  • A phone, for the student part. A computer works too, but students will use phones.
  • About 60–90 minutes. You can stop and come back whenever you like.
Everything here is practice data. You cannot break anything. The three coaching institutes, the teachers, the students and their marks are all made up. Click freely, create things, delete things, and try to confuse it. If something goes wrong, that is a useful finding, not a problem.

Part 1

What PaperFlow does

The problem it solves, and the loop that runs every time a test is written.

For teachers: setting a good test takes hours. PaperFlow makes a complete, balanced paper in seconds from a checked question bank. The teacher picks the chapters and the type of paper. The paper follows the marks pattern, spreads easy, medium and hard questions sensibly, and comes with answer keys. It can print several versions (sets) of the same paper so that neighbours cannot copy.

For students: after a test, a student opens PaperFlow on their phone and taps the questions they got wrong. That takes under a minute. PaperFlow then gives them a practice set: fresh questions on exactly those topics, at a similar level, that they have not seen before. Over several tests it builds a picture of their weak spots.

The loop:

1. Teacher sets a paperChooses chapters. Paper and answer keys are made in seconds.
→
2. Prints setsSet A, Set B… same difficulty, different order and choices.
→
3. Students write itOn paper, in class, as normal.
→
4. Student logs mistakesOn their phone, in under a minute.
→
5. Practice appearsNew questions on the same topics, with solutions.

The four kinds of people

TeacherSets papers, prints them, makes batches (class groups), and sees who has logged each test.
StudentJoins a batch with a code, logs tests, practises, and sees their weak spots. Uses a phone.
Institute ownerRuns the coaching centre's account: who is a teacher or student, which subjects each teacher teaches, and which subjects the centre uses.
PaperFlow (us)Look after the shared question bank. Every question is checked by a person before it can reach a paper.
Two things worth knowing before you start
  • Each institute is private. One coaching centre can never see another's students, papers or marks. You will see three pretend centres: Sunrise, Riverside and Hilltop.
  • The questions are samples. The Class 10 Maths questions are real maths with correct answers, but they are machine-made examples to show how things work, not the final question bank. Judge the website, and tell us separately if the question style is off.

Part 3

As a teacher

Sign in as sunrise.teacher1

3a. Set a paper (left menu → Set a paper)

Why it exists: this is the heart of PaperFlow, a whole balanced paper with answers in seconds instead of hours.

  • Under Class and subject, choose Class 10 · Mathematics. A teacher only sees the subjects the institute has assigned to them.
  • Tick a few Chapters to cover, e.g. Real Numbers, Polynomials, Quadratic Equations.
  • For the pattern (the paper's marks structure), choose Staging Unit Test (25 marks). A pattern says how many questions of each kind: 1-mark, 2-mark, 5-mark and so on. Real CBSE board patterns will come with the real bank.
  • Try the Difficulty mix (how much is easy, medium or hard) and set Printed sets to 2.
  • A full paper appears on the right. Check that the maths symbols (fractions, powers, √, π) display properly.
  • Swap a question you don't like (the ⇄ button beside it). It is replaced by another with the same marks, chapter and difficulty, so the paper stays balanced.
  • Lock a question you like, then press Generate a different paper. Locked questions stay; everything else changes. It's a quick way to keep the good bits.
  • Flag any question as wrong or badly worded (the flag button), and write a short reason. That question is pulled from everyone's papers until we have checked it. One teacher's report protects every centre. It then appears under Flagged questions in the menu, where it can be withdrawn.
  • Press Save and print. Give the paper a name and pick a batch.
  • On the print page, check you get each set on its own pages, answer keys for each set, and a sheet showing which question in Set A is which question in Set B. Sets stop copying. The mapping sheet lets a teacher mark any set with one key in mind.
  • If a setting is impossible (for example too few chapters for the pattern), read the message you get. Does it tell you clearly what to change?

3b. My papers (left menu → My papers)

Why it exists: reprint any old paper exactly, and see which students have logged it.

  • Open Staging Unit Test 1. You see the marks, the sets, and who logged, and which set they wrote.
  • If a student chose the wrong set when logging, the teacher can correct it here and their practice rebuilds. Look for that control.
  • Print everything again: it should be exactly the same paper as before.

3c. Batches (left menu → Batches)

Why it exists: a batch is a class group. Students join by typing a short code, with no lists to type in.

  • Find the 6-character join code of 10-Mathematics A. Write it down: you need it in Part 6.
  • Create a new batch. It gets its own code straight away.
  • Try rotating a code, e.g. if it was shared too widely. The old code stops working.

Part 3 · continued

Science: a Life Processes paper

Sign in as sunrise.teacher2

Why this page exists: this is the best place to judge the quality of the papers PaperFlow sets. Class 10 Science, Chapter 5 Life Processes, has a full CBSE-style question bank: about 170 questions with answers, worked solutions and marking points. It includes MCQs, assertion–reason, 2-, 3- and 5-mark questions, and case-based questions with a passage.

What's in the chapter Nine topics: what life processes are, autotrophic nutrition, heterotrophic nutrition, nutrition in human beings, respiration, transportation in human beings, transportation in plants, excretion in human beings, and excretion in plants. The questions are original, written in the CBSE board style from the NCERT syllabus; none are copied from the textbook or from past papers.
  • Set a paper → Class 10 · Science → under chapters, tick only Life Processes.
  • Pattern CBSE-style Chapter Test (40 marks): 10 one-mark (MCQ and assertion–reason), 4 two-mark, 3 three-mark, one 5-mark with an internal choice, and 2 case-based questions. A realistic class test for one chapter.
  • Then try CBSE Class 10 Science (Board), the full 80-mark board layout, from this one chapter. A hard test of the bank: 39 questions, 3 case studies and 3 either/or long answers, all from one chapter.
  • Read the paper like a teacher. Does it cover the whole chapter, or keep returning to one topic? Is the easy/medium/hard mix right? Would you be happy to hand it out?
  • Check the answer key on the print page: are the answers correct and marked the way CBSE would mark them?
  • Try Swap on a few questions and Generate a different paper a few times. Are the replacements as good as the originals?
  • Change the difficulty mix (for example 20 / 50 / 30) and see if the paper really gets harder.
  • Print 2 sets. Is Set B a fair equivalent of Set A?
The most useful feedback here Any question that is wrong, unclear, or not how CBSE asks it: use the Flag button and write why. Tell us too if anything important in the chapter is missing, or if a type of question you always set isn't there.

Other Science chapters only have placeholder questions for now, so pick Life Processes alone. The Maths chapters (Part 3a) are fully filled.

Part 4

As a student

On your phone, sign in as sunrise.student7

Why it exists: most students never look at a marked test again. PaperFlow turns each test into targeted practice while the mistakes are fresh.

4a. Log a test

  • The home screen lists the batch's tests. Staging Unit Test 1 says it needs logging. Open it.
  • Which set did you write? Pick one. Each set has the questions in a different order, so the app must know which one the student had.
  • It asks "Is this question 1 on your sheet?" and shows it. Confirm. A quick check that the student picked the right set, before they mark anything.
  • Tap three or four questions as wrong.
  • Press Save and build my practice set. Was it quick and obvious? Would a 15-year-old manage it without help?

4b. Practice (Practice tab)

  • A practice set is waiting: new questions on the same topics, none seen before.
  • Try a question, then open its solution. Solutions unlock as the student works, so they try first instead of reading the answer.

4c. Weak spots (Weak spots tab)

  • For a fuller picture, sign out and sign in as sunrise.student1, who has logged both tests. Weak spots lists the topics they keep getting wrong. Deliberately no score, percentage or rank. It's a to-do list, not a report card. Do you agree with that choice?

4d. Me, and the app on the phone

  • Me shows the student's batches, a box to enter a join code, and change password.
  • On Android Chrome, open the site a second time: Chrome should offer to Install PaperFlow. Install it: it opens like a normal app.
  • Turn on airplane mode and open the app. Tests you've already opened should still show, and trying to log should say clearly that it needs internet, without losing what you marked.
Questions to think about as a student Is anything written in words a student would not understand? Is there anything a student would want that isn't there, such as their marks, the teacher's comments or a reminder?

Part 5

As the institute owner

On the computer, sign in as sunrise.admin

Why it exists: the coaching centre controls its own people and data. The owner cannot change the questions themselves, so the question bank stays trustworthy for everyone.

5a. Overview

  • A summary of teachers, students, batches and papers. Is this the information an owner wants first?

5b. Members

  • Everyone at Sunrise is listed with their username and role. Try Filter by role.
  • Reset password for a student. You get a new temporary password, shown once, to pass on. Students forget passwords constantly. The owner can fix it without contacting us.
  • Change someone between teacher and student, then check Recent role changes records it.
  • Look at Invite someone: you invite a person by their username.

5c. Teacher subjects

  • Tick or untick which teacher teaches which subject. A teacher can only set papers and make batches for their own subjects.

5d. Subjects

  • Every class and subject is listed, including ones Sunrise can't use yet, with the reason (for example, "being reviewed now"). A subject is only switched on once the shared bank has enough checked questions for every chapter. That way a teacher never gets a thin or lopsided paper.
  • Request a subject that isn't active. We see the request on our side.

5e. Export data

  • One click downloads everything the institute owns. The centre's data is theirs. They can take it with them at any time.

5f. Privacy check

  • In a private window, sign in as riverside.teacher1. Confirm that nothing from Sunrise appears: no students, batches, papers or private questions.

Part 6

How a new person joins

Anyone can make an account, but a new account can't see anything until the institute lets them in.

Why it works this way Nobody can make themselves a teacher. A new account belongs to no institute, so a stranger with the link sees nothing. There are two ways in: a student types a batch's join code, or anyone asks the institute and the owner decides what role they get.

6a. A new teacher asks to join

  • Sign out. On the sign-in page, choose Create an account. Use your own name and make up a username, e.g. aman.test, and a password.
  • You land on a welcome page: you're signed in, but not in any institute yet. Is that page clear?
  • Ask Staging Sunrise Academy for access, with a note like "Maths teacher, joining Monday".
  • In another window, sign in as sunrise.admin. A notice says someone is asking to join. Review it, then Approve as Teacher.
  • Back in your new account, refresh: you're now in the teacher console.

6b. A new student joins with a code

  • Create another account, e.g. aman.student.
  • On the welcome page, type the join code you wrote down in 3c. You go straight into the student app, in that batch.

6c. Things to try to break

  • A wrong join code, a username that's already taken, a password that's too short. Are the messages clear?
  • As sunrise.admin, decline a request. What does the person see?

Part 7 · optional

As PaperFlow itself

The side only we use: looking after the shared question bank.

This part needs your own account to be given our access. Send Sanskar the username you made in Part 6 (e.g. aman.test) and he'll switch it on. Then sign in with it and choose Platform console.

  • Review queue: new questions wait here. Each is checked, mainly "does the answer match the question?", and approved or rejected. No question reaches a paper without a person checking it. A wrong answer key in front of a parent is the worst thing that could happen.
  • Activation: each subject shows whether the bank is thick enough to switch on. Class 10 Maths passes; Class 10 Science deliberately doesn't.
  • Institutes: the three centres, with their size.
  • Access requests and Subject requests: people asking to join, and centres asking for a subject. Riverside has asked for Science.
  • Support: look up a person (e.g. sunrise.student1) and reset their password.
  • Health and Audit log: how full the free server is, and a record of every time we looked at an institute's data. We can see institutes' data to help them, but every look is recorded.
